🚔 Police & Arrest Rights in Nigeria

What police can and cannot do, what to say if you are stopped or arrested, bail rules, detention limits, and exactly how to report abuse.

📖 8-min read ⚖ 1999 Constitution §§ 35, 36 ⚖ Police Act 2020 ⚖ ACJA 2015 🌍 English + Pidgin scripts

⚡ The 30-second version:
  1. You have the right to remain silent — use it.
  2. Ask: "Am I under arrest? What is my offence?" Police must tell you.
  3. You have the right to a lawyer and to call family.
  4. You must be charged to court within 24–48 hours, or released on bail.
  5. Bail is free. Never pay for bail.
  6. Stay calm, don't resist physically — record and report afterwards.

1. Your 6 core rights when arrested

These come mainly from Section 35 of the 1999 Constitution (right to personal liberty) and Section 36 (fair hearing), plus the Police Act 2020:

  1. Right to be told why (S.35(3)): Police must inform you promptly, in a language you understand, of the facts and grounds of your arrest.
  2. Right to remain silent (S.35(2), ACJA S.6): You cannot be forced to make a statement. Anything you say can be used against you.
  3. Right to a lawyer of your choice: And if you cannot afford one, the Legal Aid Council must help you free.
  4. Right to be charged quickly (S.35(4)–(5)): Within 24 hours (if there is a court within 40km) or 48 hours (if not) — or be released on bail.
  5. Right to dignity — no torture (S.34, Anti-Torture Act 2017): Beating, hanging, "shooting the leg", or forcing confessions is illegal.
  6. Right to bail for most offences: Especially bailable / minor offences. Bail pending trial is a constitutional presumption of innocence (S.36(5)).

2. What police CAN and CANNOT do

✅ Police CAN

  • Stop and ask questions if they reasonably suspect an offence (Police Act 2020, S.48–50)
  • Search you or your vehicle with reasonable suspicion, or with a warrant
  • Arrest without warrant if they reasonably suspect you committed an offence
  • Take your statement — but only voluntarily, with caution ("you have the right to remain silent")
  • Detain you briefly while investigating, within the 24/48-hour limit

⛔ Police CANNOT

  • Arrest you for a civil matter — debt, rent dispute, breach of contract (forbidden by Police Act 2020, S.32)
  • Arrest you in place of someone else (no "arrest mother until son comes")
  • Force you to pay for bail, fuel, "mobilisation", or writing statement
  • Search your phone/laptop without your consent or a court order — challenge this
  • Beat, torture, or parade you before trial
  • Hold you beyond 24–48 hours without a court order (remand order)

3. If you are stopped or searched — step by step

  1. Stay calm and respectful. Keep hands visible. Do not run or argue aggressively.
  2. Ask politely: "Good afternoon officer, please what is the reason for this stop?" Note their name tag, uniform number, vehicle plate, time and location.
  3. You may film in public. There is no law against recording police in public. If they object, keep your phone recording in your pocket.
  4. For phone searches: You can say: "Officer, I do not consent to a search of my phone without a warrant." Say it calmly — if they force it, comply physically but note it as a violation to report.
  5. Never sign what you haven't read. Read every statement before signing. You may write "I decline to make a statement at this time."
What to say (English):
"Officer, am I under arrest or am I free to go? Please tell me my offence. I wish to remain silent and call my lawyer/family."

Wetin to talk (Pidgin):
"Officer, abeg I wan know — una don arrest me abi I fit go? Abeg tell me wetin be my offence. I no wan talk anything for now, I wan call my lawyer/family."

4. At the station & detention limits

  • Cautionary statement: Before writing, police must caution you. You can refuse to write. If you write, keep it short, truthful, and sign each page.
  • Detention clock: S.35(4)–(5) Constitution — charge or release within 24 hrs (court within 40km radius) or 48 hrs (no court nearby). Any longer needs a court remand order.
  • Access: You are entitled to see a lawyer, doctor (if sick/injured), and family. Denial is a violation.
  • Detainee rights under ACJA (Lagos/FCT/Federal cases): humane treatment, medical care, and a central criminal records check — not indefinite holding for "investigation".
⚠️ Never: pay "bail money", sign a blank paper, agree to be a "witness" against yourself, or accept to pay the complainant just to be released if you are innocent — get a lawyer first.

5. Bail is free — how it works

  • Police bail (administrative bail) for minor/bailable offences: granted at the station, usually with a surety (a person who guarantees you will return). No fee.
  • What surety needs: Usually valid ID, proof of address, sometimes passport photo. A surety should NOT pay money to police.
  • Court bail for serious offences: your lawyer applies to the court. Conditions may include a sum and sureties — still no bribe.
  • If police say "bail is ₦50,000" — that is extortion. Politely decline, note names, and report (see below).

6. If your rights are violated — report it

  1. Collect evidence immediately: names/numbers, station, date/time, photos of injuries, medical report from a government hospital, video, witnesses' contacts.
  2. Report to:
    • Police Service Commission (PSC) — complaints against police misconduct
    • National Human Rights Commission (NHRC) — human rights violations
    • Public Complaints Commission (Ombudsman)
    • State Police Complaints Response Unit (CRU) / Officer in charge of your division
    • Legal Aid Council of Nigeria — free lawyer if you can't afford one
  3. Write it down: Keep a one-page timeline: what happened, when, where, who, evidence. RightAware's Get Help form can help you structure this.
  4. Go to court if needed: You can file a Fundamental Rights enforcement suit at the Federal or State High Court for illegal arrest/detention (with a lawyer's help).
